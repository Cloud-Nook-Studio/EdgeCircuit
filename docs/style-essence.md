# Style Essence

This is the canonical visual and interaction reference for the product. Read it
before making any user-visible design change. Use
[`design-direction.md`](design-direction.md) for the more detailed visual
grammar and implementation rules.

## The feeling

The app should feel like a premium cognitive instrument: calm, precise,
purposeful, and quietly advanced.

Its intelligence should come from proportion, rhythm, clarity, and behavior—not
from visual complexity. The high-level science-fiction cue is an elegant object
engineered around unfamiliar but internally coherent geometry.

The design balances four tensions:

- **Futuristic, not theatrical.**
- **Executive, not corporate.**
- **Sharp, not aggressive.**
- **Intelligent, not complicated.**

## The signature

Fivefold radial balance is the product's underlying spatial signature. It may
appear in target placement, faint construction lines, icon composition, or
motion paths. It should be sensed before it is consciously noticed.

The EdgeCircuit mark is a compact asymmetric blueprint assembly derived from the
Pulse Path target family. A translucent mineral body, solid outer frame,
displaced dashed contour, internal brace, dotted guide, and one restrained gold
index point make it recognizable at header scale. It must feel related to the
game geometry without becoming an exact game target. It is deliberately
abstract: never turn it into an M, a V, a brain, a head silhouette, a gemstone,
or a four-square app tile.

Shapes belong to one coherent, predominantly angular family: faceted hexagons,
diamonds, pentagons, prisms, kites, beveled blocks, peaks, and restrained
shields. Favor mathematically legible silhouettes with purposeful bevels and
diagonal cuts: hexagons, octagons, kites, prisms, trapezoids, pentagons, and
restrained shields. Their outer boundaries should read as engineered geometry,
not asteroids, rocks, or irregular organic fragments. Variation belongs in
proportion, orientation, and mathematically legible silhouette rather than
noisy perimeter points. Pulse Path markers read as asymmetric blueprint
assemblies: a restrained translucent body, solid outer frame, displaced dashed
contour, structural braces, and a dotted reference axis. Their depth comes from
overlapping line systems, never three contrasting gemstone facets or generic
flat icon fills. Avoid mixing in soft capsules, circles, or generic rounded
squares that make the field feel like an unrelated shape sampler.

Faint connecting lines should reveal an engineered structure behind the
constellation. They should read as a deliberate center-out radial system, not a
random web. The network may carry an extremely slow traveling signal or
breathing change in opacity. Pulse Path forms may float ten-to-sixteen pixels
on independent, smooth zero-gravity loops while their invisible hit targets and
visible line anchors remain fixed. This makes every fragment feel lightly
tethered to the radial structure rather than loose on the screen.

The home field should read like a restrained technical blueprint rather than a
collection of simple floating shapes. Structured, low-opacity circuit
scaffolding may extend across the field through nested traces, radial branches,
small junctions, calibration ticks, short offset fragments, and sparse
one-to-two-pixel signal points. This layer must feel deliberately routed and
atmospheric rather than like random decoration, a starfield, or a busy HUD. The
playable game nodes remain its dominant orbital bodies. Do not add speculative
**TBD** stations or empty capacity markers; unimplemented games do not occupy
the home field.

Playable exercises that are not part of the current Daily Circuit float in the
quiet constellation space around the route. They remain launchable game choices
rather than disabled placeholders and retain the user's exact local placement.
This surrounding field is a user-controlled holding area, not a bottom tray,
list, or placeholder field. Give each off-circuit card its own restrained
zero-gravity loop: roughly three to six pixels of elliptical drift, less than
half a degree of rotation, and a varied nine-to-eleven-second phase. Animate
only the visible inner shell so the saved position, hit target, and drag origin
remain stable. Pause the drift during hover, keyboard focus, and dragging.

The home screen extends that signature into one dominant orbital navigation
instrument. Its primary Daily Circuit is a sharply and deliberately beveled
asymmetric polygon, not a circle or ordinary ellipse. Its intelligence comes
from layered construction: nested perimeter traces, radial spokes, junction
points, and mixed solid, dashed, and dotted line weights. At its center, the
Daily Circuit hub is a compact black-hole instrument. Preserve its black
event-horizon ring and surround it with several fine, imperfect blue-white
sketch traces. Those broken, slightly displaced lines should vary in weight and
dash rhythm like light being pulled around the core. A single stationary brass
anchor circle separates the black event horizon from those exterior traces.
Give that anchor restrained polished depth through a bright upper edge, dark
lower edge, and faint warm reflection. A finer steel-white event-horizon rim
sits inside it. Both solid rings remain fixed; only the exterior sketch traces
rotate. Use three close planes in bright white, soft white, and steel-white;
mix clockwise and counterclockwise motion at roughly 14, 18, and 23 seconds per
revolution. Keep the halo tucked close to the anchor rather than floating as a
separate atom. Nothing rotates or
draws across the black interior. Broad brass rails,
steampunk orbit hardware, and dense angular braces do not belong in this
nucleus. The nucleus never turns green; green
remains reserved for completed game nodes, correct feedback, and genuinely
strong task scores. Only the exterior halo traces may counter-rotate almost imperceptibly,
but they should read as soft gravitational fuzz rather than atomic or
Dyson-sphere machinery. A
broad dashed-gold signal segment and a thinner steel-blue counter-segment
travel around the route. Sparse one-to-two-pixel white signal points may
traverse the wider screen on long, slow, fading paths. It should feel digital,
spacefaring, and slick without becoming a starfield or dense sci-fi HUD. The
game choices remain the strongest interactive surfaces.

The fixed black-hole hub is also the single **Initiate Circuit** action. Its
visible copy is only the two-line `Initiate / Circuit` label: do not add a
`Daily circuit` eyebrow, badge fraction, completed/total game fraction, or
duplicate header control. Completed green nodes already communicate circuit
progress. Initiating runs the selected circuit games in saved order at their
current defaults, preserving each game's three-round feedback and advancing
directly between exercises. Exit to home deliberately ends the sequence.

Game choices on the home screen are medium Pulse-style blueprint nodes, not
dashboard panels or tiny chips. Their shells remain restrained while each
game uses a bespoke, immediately recognizable vector illustration occupying
roughly one-third of the node's usable width. These illustrations share the
solid, dashed, dotted, and indexed blueprint line grammar; never reduce them to
tiny generic primitive marks or Unicode symbols. The seven implemented
exercises form the available game library. The user's chosen subset travels
slowly around the asymmetric **Daily Circuit** route at even separation, while
the remaining playable exercises float in the surrounding constellation.
Circuit membership may be changed by dragging a game card between the route and
surrounding field, with an equally clear keyboard add/remove control. Dropping
on the route sets that game's starting phase; dropping outside it preserves the
chosen free position. Treat the nested inner and outer traces as one generous
drop band rather than requiring pixel-perfect contact with the visible stroke.
The dragged game owns the requested route point; if that point would overlap a
neighbor, nearby nodes redistribute by the minimum distance needed while
preserving their circular order. During the gesture, a high-fidelity copy of the complete
blueprint node stays visibly attached to the original grab point under the
pointer while the source rests faintly in place. Do not substitute a connector
line, drag banner, generic browser ghost, or detached cursor badge. Playable
nodes are approximately one-quarter smaller than
the earlier 220-by-165-pixel shells so the circuit stays open and legible. Nodes
orbit without spinning: every title remains perfectly horizontal, and hovering,
focusing, or dragging a node pauses the set for easy selection. The icon, title,
play count, and success readout remain registered to the same fixed coordinates
inside the blueprint shell before, during, and after hover.
Completing an exercise turns only its blueprint shell to a small new resting
angle and fills the complete clipped polygon field with a clear muted green,
retaining gold and pale-green construction traces. This full-green completion
state appears for the day only after the player finishes all three rounds at
70% session accuracy or better. A
completed attempt below that threshold still increments the play count and
updates the success readout, but the node remains blue/gold and may be replayed
to clear it. Do not place speculative future slots anywhere on the home field.
Each playable
node carries one clear circular play counter directly beneath the game name.
The count is larger, white, and paired with `play` or `plays`; an unplayed game
reads `0`. Directly beneath it, show mean completed-session task success as a
percentage in the same larger, pure-white metric style. With no completed
session, show `—% success` rather than manufacturing a zero. A tiny muted-green
datum may identify a strong result, but the metric text itself stays white.
Visually center the copy column within the asymmetric shell, which requires a
clear leftward registration from the rectangular card center. Keep the title
visually dominant and reserve enough width to show the full game name and word
`success` without clipping. On green completed cards, both the play number and
its `play`/`plays` label remain pure white.
Register the play-count row independently from the left-shifted title column:
push it right far enough to leave a visible gap from the game illustration,
while keeping the complete row inside the polygon.
Never describe this task-specific percentage as a generalized cognitive score.

After an exactly recalled Pulse Path round, illuminate only the center-out
radial tethers belonging to the recalled targets. Resolve them in muted green
with one short traveling dash, then advance normally. A missed round never
lights these lines.

The interface is not a keypad, dashboard grid, arcade cabinet, or collection of
decorative cards. Elements should feel placed in space, with generous negative
space and a clear center of attention.

## Surface and color

- Near-black navy creates depth without looking black.
- Muted mineral blue-gray carries structure and inactive elements.
- Inactive targets may vary across a narrow family of mineral-blue tones so
  the field has life without becoming color-coded.
- Deep, restrained gold marks attention and active signals.
- Muted mineral green is reserved for successful resolution, primarily the
  shared confirmation check used by Pulse Path and Digit Hold.
- Desaturated steel-blue lines may reveal underlying geometry at low opacity.
- The 48-pixel construction grid remains subtly visible in both themes: use
  quiet light steel lines on dark navy and quiet slate lines on the light paper
  field. It should orient the composition without becoming graph paper.
- In light mode, playable vector shells remain exposed directly on the field.
  Never place an opaque or translucent rectangular backing behind their clipped
  blueprint geometry.
- Pulse Path polygons and the EdgeCircuit mark use translucent mineral bodies and layered blueprint
  traces. Combine a solid perimeter, dashed offset contour, solid brace, and
  dotted guide at distinct low-contrast weights. Avoid opaque icon blocks,
  glossy bevels, central highlights, and multi-plane gemstone shading. Keep the
  mark distinct through its compact proportions and single gold index point.
- Color is sparse. Gold is a signal, not decoration.
- Avoid decorative page gradients, rainbow coding, neon cyan, glossy glass
  effects, ornamental glow, and internal shading that makes a game target read
  like a toy gemstone. Complexity belongs in precise line overlap, not color
  variation.

## Type and language

- Major moments may use an editorial serif with authority and warmth.
- Controls and metrics use a compact, highly legible sans serif.
- Monospace is reserved for digit strings or genuinely technical readouts.
- Copy is brief, direct, and composed. Never sound breathless, gamified, or
  clinical.
- The home screen has no marketing headline. Do not add “Sharpen your edge,”
  “Choose your focus,” or a substitute. The ring labels itself **“Daily
  circuit”** at its center. Game titles remain on one horizontal line; the
  second word—**Path**, **Hold**, **Shift**, **Sweep**, and **Match**—uses
  restrained gold.
- Prefer “Watch the path” over explanatory paragraphs.

## Motion and response

Motion should communicate state with the minimum necessary gesture.

- Use one controlled target pulse, reveal, or transition.
- Ambient motion belongs primarily in the faint structural network. Pulse Path
  may add a very slow, small tethered drift to each fragment; the anchor and
  connecting line never move.
- Tethered forms translate ten-to-sixteen pixels and rotate by roughly one
  degree over a seven-to-ten-second loop. Their phase and direction vary
  so the field feels suspended rather than synchronized.
- Pulse Path leaves a 1.45-second settling beat after a round begins before the
  first shape activates. Later pulse spacing stays crisp and unchanged.
- Keep timing crisp but unhurried.
- Never wobble, bounce, shower particles, or spin the game nodes. They may
  translate slowly clockwise around the Daily Circuit while staying upright.
  One complete playable-node route takes approximately 116 seconds. Route
  signals and the dominant broken construction planes flow counterclockwise,
  opposite the playable nodes; the two atomic particles around the hub retain
  independent orbital motion. Two or three tiny restrained signal points may also traverse
  the angular perimeter itself at different speeds and directions so route
  motion remains unmistakable without becoming decorative.
- Pause the orbital set during a card drag. Use one restrained route or reserve
  highlight to confirm the valid drop target. Keep the node's complete shell and
  artwork visible under the pointer without a connector line or drag label; do
  not add spring physics, exaggerated lift, or a celebratory insertion animation.
- Feedback should settle the interface rather than excite it.

## Interaction character

Every screen has one dominant action. Touch targets may be generous, but visible
controls remain visually light. Progress is clear without pressure; response
speed is not treated as intelligence.

Active games are intentionally disposable. All exercises provide a clear
**Exit to home** action, never Save, Save & exit, Pause, or Resume. Exiting
discards the unfinished three-round session. Completed-session history and the
daily Practice Charge may persist, but an unfinished exercise never appears as
a saved item on the home screen.

Daily Circuit membership and placement are locally persisted preferences,
independent of daily completion. A player can drag any playable game between the
circuit and the quiet surrounding constellation; an off-circuit game remains
launchable from its card and may be repositioned freely. Moving a game in either
direction never deletes its completed-task history or Practice Charge
contribution. The central hub counts only selected circuit games: its numerator
is the number of included games completed today and its denominator is the
number included. If no games are selected, the hub reads **Standby** rather than
treating `0/0` as complete.

Muted green is displayed only on the circuit. A floating off-circuit game keeps
its blue-gray shell even after a qualifying completed session. Completion state
must not split from performance history: finishing all three rounds at the
clearance threshold establishes that game's completion for the local day.
If the completed game is on, or later returns to, the circuit, its full shell
resolves to unmistakable muted green.

Per-game history is also local and independent of the daily reset. Increment a
game's play count only when its full three-round session completes. Its accuracy
badge is the mean of completed-session task accuracy for that game; exiting an
unfinished session changes neither value.

Pointer dragging must have a complete keyboard equivalent. Each playable card
exposes a concise **Add to circuit** or **Move to reserve** control according to
its current state, preserves ordinary keyboard launch behavior, and announces
the resulting location. The drop zones and controls stay visually subordinate
to the game choices until interaction makes them relevant.

The header includes a compact daily **Practice Charge** battery. It is a
practice-quality meter, not a cognitive score. Its 100 points combine circuit
coverage (45), progress through three planned rounds per selected circuit game
(25), mean task accuracy (20), and accuracy-gated response pace (10). Accuracy
and pace phase in across six measured rounds so one fast perfect response
cannot dominate the meter. A larger circuit therefore creates a larger planned
workload rather than free charge; an empty circuit holds at zero in standby.
The meter records completed reps, resets on the next local calendar day, and
persists through refreshes. Never describe it as brain power, intelligence,
readiness, or health, and never attach streak penalties or loss anxiety to it.

The gold orbit immediately around the central hub is a compact daily
performance dashboard, not a reward shelf. Four small readouts attach to its
cardinal points: **Accuracy** is mean task accuracy across completed rounds;
**Pace** is mean response time per expected item; **Recall** is mean task
accuracy for Pulse Path and Digit Hold; and **Rounds** is the number completed
today. Empty measures show an em dash rather than manufactured data. These are
task diagnostics, not measures of intelligence, readiness, health, or transfer.
They reset with Practice Charge on the next local day.
Keep these readouts narrow and annotation-like; target roughly 56 CSS pixels
wide, allowing Pace about 62 pixels for its unit. They must never expand into
wide dashboard cards or visually overpower the central circuit.
The fixed central hub may grow to roughly 126 CSS pixels on desktop so
`Daily circuit`, its count, status, and badge tally remain comfortably inside.

Daily achievements remain available but do not occupy four large orbiting
medals. **First loop**, **Momentum**, and **Full circuit** recognize 3, 9, and
15 completed rounds. **Precision** recognizes at least 90% mean task accuracy
across six or more rounds. The hub shows one quiet earned-badge tally; detailed
badge progress may live in a later diagnostics view. Achievements persist for
the current local day and never create streaks, penalties, confetti, coins, or
claims about intelligence.

Pulse Path begins with one restrained path-length choice from two to eight
steps. Three steps is the default, and the chosen length remains fixed across
all three rounds so the session has one clear cognitive demand. The complete
constellation remaps between rounds: silhouette selection, shape-to-position
mapping, and layout all change after feedback, while the field stays perfectly
stable throughout each individual watch-and-recall iteration.

Rule Shift is a three-decision interference-control exercise. A geometric
signal has both a screen position and an arrow direction; the active rule
alternates between those attributes. The user chooses left or right according
to the named rule while ignoring the competing attribute. Report performance
on this task only—never “executive function,” intelligence, or workplace
readiness.

Signal Sweep is a three-round selective visual-search exercise. A setup
control offers four, six, eight, or ten candidate signals, with six as the
default. Each round presents one blueprint cue among that field. Exactly one
candidate matches the cue's contour, orientation, and index mark; every other
candidate is a deliberate near-match. One selection resolves the round. Report only
task-specific search accuracy and response pace, never a generalized attention,
intelligence, or workplace-readiness score.

Vector Match is a three-round spatial-discrimination exercise. Each round pairs
an asymmetric blueprint figure with a transformed comparison and asks whether
the comparison is the same figure under rotation or a mirror reflection.
Orientation, displaced contours, and index marks make reflection meaningfully
different from rotation. Report only performance on this task, never spatial
intelligence, general reasoning, or cognitive-health claims.

Trace Pair is a three-round relational-matching exercise. Each round presents
six open blueprint assemblies around a loose circular constellation, with one
quiet radial spoke from each assembly to a central junction. Exactly two share
the same internal connection topology while their outer frames, datum marks,
and rotations differ. The player selects those two assemblies directly; there
are no face-down cards,
duplicate pictures, or memorized tile locations. Each visible assembly follows
its own restrained six-to-nine-pixel zero-gravity loop with less than one degree
of rotation, while its larger selectable hit area remains fixed. Vary duration,
direction, and phase so the field feels suspended rather than synchronized.
When the correct pair resolves, illuminate only its two radial spokes in muted
green with a restrained gold transition.
Report only pair-selection accuracy and response pace on this task.

Name Recall is a three-round associative-memory exercise. Each round presents
three distinct, recognizably human professional portraits paired with first
names for 4.5 seconds. Faces use consistent framing and lighting while retaining
clear differences in age, features, hair, and presentation; never substitute
abstract geometric identity glyphs. After a 650-millisecond blank retention beat, one identity returns and
the player selects its associated name from the same three-name set. The entire
identity/name mapping changes every round. Report only name-association accuracy
and response pace on this task; never claim improvement in general memory,
social ability, or professional effectiveness.

Choice-based exercises use a visible five-second pace-bonus hourglass. The
hourglass is an optional scoring window, not a deadline: after it expires the
round remains fully playable and correctness remains more important than speed.

For memory tasks, transitions should reinforce the cognitive act rather than
create extra screens. Digit Hold uses a high-visibility vertical timing rail
immediately beside the number: a large hourglass descends along the rail and
the seconds readout sits below. It remains subordinate to the digit string but
must be unmistakable. The fixed exposure is 1.3 seconds and begins with one
restrained reveal chime. After exposure, use a deliberate half-second blank
retention interval and then recall. Correct/incorrect feedback appears directly
on the recall surface as a refined check or X before the next round advances.
Digit Hold locks and evaluates automatically as soon as the selected number of
digits has been entered; it never requires Enter or a submit button. Correct
answers across the games use a faceted muted-green check plate surrounded by two
incomplete orbital segments: a brighter outer segment and a thinner,
steel-green inner segment moving briefly in the opposite direction. Never draw
a complete circular success ring.

The user should feel:

1. oriented immediately;
2. focused on one cognitive act;
3. respected rather than manipulated;
4. ready to return tomorrow.

## Never drift toward

- Phone-pad grids or numbered tiles.
- Wonky, organic, or novelty silhouettes.
- Busy sci-fi interfaces, indiscriminate circuitry, HUD decoration, or fake
  telemetry.
- Generic wellness pastels.
- Casino-like rewards, streak anxiety, or childish celebration.
- Dense enterprise dashboards.
- Direct copies of characters, props, logos, or production designs from
  existing intellectual property.

## The removal test

Before adding a visual element, ask:

1. Does it clarify attention, action, progress, or feedback?
2. Does it reinforce the product's geometric signature?
3. Would the screen feel less intelligent if it were removed?

If the answer is no, remove it.
